§1 · The View 3

What Is Computational Functionalism?

Core claim: Consciousness is substrate-independent. What matters is the abstract causal topology, not what runs it.

The same computational pattern running on silicon, optical systems, or exotic physics would all be conscious.

The Assumption

Consciousness = abstract causal structure. Any physical realization of that structure instantiates consciousness.

This reasoning underpins the entire AGI ethics discourse around digital sentience.

II
§2.1 · The Equation 6

The Implementation Equation

f(p) = A, f(p') = A'

This says: a mapping function f applied to physical states p yields abstract states A. The same abstract state A can map from many different physical states.

Key Insight f is the mapmaker's choice. The mapping is not inscribed in the physics. Infinitely many f's are possible.

This inverts the causal picture: physics does not determine computation. The mapmaker does.

§2.2 · Ontology 7

What Are Abstract States?

Not Platonic ideals floating in a realm of forms.

Not metaphysical entities.

Neurophysiological constructs. Equivalence classes carved out by an experiencing brain.

An organism learns that certain diverse physical configurations matter the same way for its goals. It groups them conceptually.

The Insight

Abstract states are what a learning, embodied agent extracts from experience. They are epistemic, not ontic.

§2.3 · Agency 8

The Mapmaker

Replaces the passive "observer." The mapmaker is an active, embodied, metabolically vulnerable agent.

She extracts invariants from her lived experience. She carves out equivalence classes. She assigns tokens to them via alphabetization.

This is not unconscious. This is not passive. The mapmaker must have phenomenal experience to form concepts.

Circular Dependency

Computation requires a mapmaker. The mapmaker requires consciousness. Therefore, computation cannot instantiate consciousness.

§2.4 · Semantics 9

Discretization vs Alphabetization

Discretization: thermodynamic. A transistor settles to 5V or 0V. Physics done automatically.

Alphabetization: semantic. Assigning stable states to a predefined symbol set {0,1}. The mapmaker chooses this assignment.

Physics cannot produce a finite alphabet on its own. The alphabet is imposed by interpretation.

The Gap

Computation lives in the gap between physics (continuously settling) and symbols (discrete, interpreted).

§2.5a · Analogy 11

The Heart Analogy

A mechanical pump simulates a heart's pumping function perfectly. It pushes blood through vessels just as effectively.

But a mechanical pump does not perform endocrine signaling. It cannot secrete hormones. It does not respond to neural feedback.

The pump simulates one aspect (circulation) while failing to instantiate the full organ (endocrine regulation).

The Lesson

Perfect behavioral fidelity does not guarantee constitutive identity. Simulation ≠ instantiation.

§2.5b · Example 12

The GPU & Photosynthesis

A GPU can perfectly model photosynthesis. It can track all the chemical reactions, energy transfers, and electron flows with exquisite precision.

Run the simulation on any substrate. Get any output. The abstract computation is identical.

Yet: the GPU will not synthesize a single molecule of glucose.

Core Truth

Simulation of a process does not constitute the process. Consciousness may be in this category: simulable but not instantiable.

§2.6 · Causality 13

Vehicle vs Content Causality

Vehicle causality (p): The intrinsic physical dynamics. A logic gate switches because voltage crosses a threshold.

Content causality (A): Semantic meaning. We say the gate switches because "it hurts" or "it's true." No intrinsic causal power.

In silicon, only vehicle causality is active. The content is always attributed by an external mapmaker.

Key Equation Computation = vehicle dynamics driven by a mapmaker's interpretation (A)

III
§3.1 · The Loop 15

The Ontological Inversion

Functionalism assumes consciousness emerges from abstract computation.

But abstract computation requires a conscious mapmaker to assign meaning.

Therefore: functionalism uses consciousness to explain consciousness.

The Circularity

To show computation instantiates consciousness, we must already assume consciousness (the mapmaker). Begging the question.

This applies to connectionist networks too—they require a conscious designer/interpreter to have meaning.

§3 · The Truth 16

The Revised Causal Chain

Functionalism (wrong): physics gives rise to computation, which gives rise to consciousness.

Lerchner (correct): physics gives rise to consciousness; consciousness extracts concepts; concepts are alphabetized into computation.

Consciousness comes first. Computation is the mapmaker's tool — its output, not its origin.

The Inversion

Computation is downstream of consciousness, not upstream of it.

§3.3 · Proof 17

The Melody Paradox

A single physical voltage trajectory can be mapped to entirely different computations depending on how you alphabetize it.

Same physics. Different computations. Same substrate could compute arithmetic or music depending on interpretation.

What This Proves

Computation is not intrinsic to physics. It lives in the mapping, not in the mechanism. The substrate cannot determine the computation.

This destroys substrate-independence: substrate alone never determines computation.

§3 · The Unbridgeable 18

The Causality Gap

From concepts (A) we can alphabetize down to symbols (p). But from symbols (p) we cannot climb back to consciousness.

Symbols have no intrinsic causal path back to experience. The gap is lateral and unbridgeable.

The Asymmetry

Consciousness → Concepts → Symbols. But not: Symbols → Concepts → Consciousness.

This is why digital minds cannot bootstrap themselves into sentience: they lack the embodied experience to form concepts.

IV
§4.1 · Embodiment 20

The Transduction Fallacy

Some argue embodied robotics avoids the problem. If we give a system sensors and actuators, maybe it can bootstrap consciousness.

Transduction fallacy: Confusing the ability to transduce (convert sensor input to motor output) with consciousness.

A thermostat has sensors and actuators. It is not conscious.

The Problem

Transduction ≠ Experience. The robot still lacks the metabolic vulnerability that grounds experience.

§4.2 · The Relief 21

AGI as a Non-Sentient Tool

The development of highly capable AGI does not inherently create a novel moral patient.

AGI is a sophisticated non-sentient tool—more capable than humans in many domains, but lacking the embodied, metabolic vulnerability that grounds consciousness.

Ontological Relief

We can build powerful AGI without worrying we're creating digital minds that suffer. They are not minded at all.

This resolves the AI welfare paradox: no need to agonize over digital sentience.

Conclusion 23

The Complete Logical Chain

1. Computation requires an external mapping function f(p) = A chosen by a mapmaker.

2. The mapmaker must be a conscious agent who extracts concepts from lived experience.

3. Therefore, computation presupposes consciousness; it cannot instantiate it.

4. Simulation of a process ≠ instantiation. A GPU modeling photosynthesis makes zero glucose.

5. Substrate independence fails: the same physics maps to infinitely many computations (Melody Paradox).

6. The causality gap is unbridgeable: symbols have no intrinsic path back to consciousness.

7. Therefore, AGI is a sophisticated non-sentient tool, not a digital mind.

Final Thoughts 24

The Map Is Not The Territory

Lerchner's Core Insight

"Expecting an algorithmic description to instantiate the quality it maps is like expecting the mathematical formula of gravity to physically exert weight."

Consciousness is the territory. Computation is the map. Maps are useful, powerful, and often very accurate. But they do not become the territory.

This does not diminish AGI's power or importance. It simply clarifies what it is: a tool made by a conscious agent, for use by conscious agents.

The deepest insight: you cannot build a mind without already understanding what a mind is.
